Personal project, sales analysis

Superstore Sales Analysis: Discount vs Profit

Why did Furniture sell almost as much as Technology but earn a 2.49% margin (Technology: 17.4%)? I split 9,994 orders (2014–2017) by discount level in SQL and found where the profit disappears.

  • Average profit per order: +66.9 with no discount, +26.5 up to 20%, −97.18 above 20%.
  • 1,393 orders with discounts above 20% lost 135,376 in total.
  • Tables and Bookcases were the loss-makers; Central gave big discounts most often (27.68% of orders) and had the lowest profit.
Transactions9,994
Profit margin12.5%

Correlation, not proof of cause: heavily discounted products may already have thin margins. Data is the public Kaggle Superstore sample.

Personal project, regression

Medical Insurance Cost Regression

Does a right-skewed cost distribution call for a Gamma model? I compared linear regression and Gamma GLMs on 1,338 insurance records, using 30 random 80:20 splits to compare test error.

  • Plain linear regression beat the Gamma GLM (mean RMSE 6,172 vs 7,964).
  • Adding a smoker × obesity interaction cut RMSE to 4,601 (R² 0.86).
  • Obesity barely changes costs for non-smokers, but nearly doubles them for smokers (21,363 vs 41,558).
Records1,338
R²0.86
Test RMSE4,601

Residual plots still show structure the model misses, and the obesity cut-off (BMI 30) was chosen after looking at the data, so the error gain is a little optimistic. Observational Kaggle data, so correlation only.
